The flock is not as cohesive as it was before the coordinated-turn logic. What changes might help the birds stay more aligned with each other?

> ### Why cohesion dropped

> * Slower, rate-capped turning. Turns are now limited to 𝑔 tan⁡(60°)/𝑣, about 3.4 rad/s at 5 m/s. A bird also has to roll before it can turn. Steering that used to snap to the desired heading now lags, so birds overshoot or fall behind the group.
> * Steering is relative to heading. Every Think contribution is a heading delta, and the direction term in sum carries weight 1. Cohesion and alignment, which are the terms that hold the flock together, compete with the bird's own heading and with the center, climb and altitude terms.
> * MinSpeed is above StallSpeed. The floor is 2.75 and stall is 2.1, so stall steering effectively never triggers. All birds also cruise at 3–3.25 m/s, so followers can't catch up much.
> Speed is only matched by accident. Nothing steers speed toward the neighbors' speed, and the slope term makes birds in a climb or dive speed up or slow down independently.

> ### Changes that should help

> 1. Weight the flock terms. Multiply toNeighbor and alignment by a constant (try 1.5–3) in the sum in Think. You can also reduce the weight of the direction inertia term. This is the cheapest change.
> 2. Scale cohesion by distance. Make toNeighbor stronger the farther the target is from the bird. For example, multiply by clamp(dist / 5, 0.5, 3). Close birds then only align, and distant ones pull in hard.
> 3. Average neighbor directions. alignment currently follows only the nearest bird. Average the directions of all found neighbors (the same loop that averages positions) for a smoother, more consistent heading signal.
> 4. Add speed matching. Relax PreferredSpeed toward the neighbor average, or add a term that speeds birds up when the target is far ahead and slows them when it is close. This addresses the speed-spread cause.
> 5. Reduce competing behaviors. The center pull and the climb-spreading term (ClimbStrength) fight cohesion. Fewer birds entering the spreading climb, or a weaker ClimbStrength, helps.
> 6. Narrow the neighbor filters. MinNeighborAlignment is cos 90° (anything not heading backward). Going back to cos 45° as requested earlier avoids chasing birds flying across your path. A larger NeighborCount or a smaller NeighborRange changes the group-size scale.
> 7. Loosen turn limits. Raising Gravity (flatter banks and slower turns) isn't helpful here. Consider lowering it, or raising MaxBank to 70–75°, so birds can follow tighter flock curves.
