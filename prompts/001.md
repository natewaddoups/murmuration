We're going to simulate a flock of birds in three dimensions. Initially there will be 100 birds, but that number will change depending on performance.

The ground is the XY plane, and Z is up. Units are metric (kilograms and meters).

Draw a green square to represent the ground, 100 meters on each side, centered at the origin.

Place the camera at [-50, -50, 25], aimed at [0,0,25], with a field of view wide enough to see the other 3 corners of the ground plane square.

Initially the birds will be rendered as a set of four tetrahedrons. The two 'wing' tetrahedrons have a base that is a triangle 1cm tall and 5cm wide. The far point of the wing tetrahedrons will be 30cm from the base. The bases will be coplanar at the center of the bird. The 'head' tetrahedron will have a base 3cm wide and 2cm tall, with the far point 10cm from the base. The base of the head tetrahedron will be at the center of the bird. The tail tetrahedron will have a base 0.5cm tall and 5cm wide, located 10cm behind the bird, with the tip of the tetrahedron at the center of the bird.

Initially, a bird's state will consist of:
* A direction vector - initialized with random values, normalized to a length of 1.0
* An "up" vector - initialized with X=0, Y=0, Z=1
* A position vector - initialized with random values from -50 to +50 (meters).
* A speed scalar - initially a random number from 15 to 20 (meters per second).
* A mass scalar - initialized with a random number from 0.25 kilograms to 0.5 kilograms.

At each time step, update each bird's direction vectors by adding two angular adjustments to the bird's previous direction vector. The first adjustment will be computed by steering toward the point at 0,0,25. The second adjustment will be computed by steering toward a point that is a distance of 1.0 from the nearest bird. Both adjustement angles will be capped so that a bird with velocity 15 can change direction by 180 degrees per second, and a bird with velocity 20 can change direction by 135 degrees per second.

After updating the direction vectors, compute the turn radius in meters, based on the speed and the direction change. Update the bird's "up" vector so that gravity and centripetal force are balanced.

(Speeds will remain constant for now.)

After updating directions, update the birds' positions based on their new directions and their speeds.

Then render the scene.

Repeat indefinitely.
